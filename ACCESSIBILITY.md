# Accessibility statement

Plainspoken aims to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA. High-contrast mode aims for AAA colour contrast (7:1).

## What you can adjust (Settings)

- **Colour:** light, dark or match your device; high contrast or match your device's contrast setting; five colour themes. Every combination is checked for contrast on every change (check C40).
- **Text:** size from 90% to 150% (on top of browser zoom and your device's text size); Atkinson Hyperlegible, Lexend or your device's font; relaxed line, letter and word spacing (WCAG 1.4.12).
- **Interaction:** larger buttons (48px); an extra-visible focus outline; reduce motion; hide rewards and celebrations.
- **Layout:** works from 320px wide phones to wide desktops, in portrait and landscape, without sideways scrolling (tested at 8 screen sizes at 100% and 150% text). Tablets in landscape and desktops get a side navigation rail.

## Built in

- A skip link to the main content, a focusable main region, page titles for each view, and the page language set to English (UK).
- Changes in settings are announced to screen readers.
- Tab strips work with arrow, Home and End keys.
- Windows high-contrast (forced colours) mode keeps borders, focus and progress bars visible.
- Colour is never the only signal: pills carry text, quiz answers show a tick and "Right" or "Not quite", charts carry text alternatives.
- Pages print cleanly for cue cards, briefs and lessons.

## Known gaps

- Not yet tested with the full range of screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, TalkBack) or with switch and voice-control software.
- Dictation and live listening depend on your device and browser; Welsh speech recognition depends on browser support.
- The decorative artwork and spectrum view are simple SVGs; the Progress tables carry the same information as text.
- Lexend, like the default fonts, loads from Google Fonts.

Report a problem by opening an issue (there is an accessibility form). Last reviewed: 28 October 2026.
